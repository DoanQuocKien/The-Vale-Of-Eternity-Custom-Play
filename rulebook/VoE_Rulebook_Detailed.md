# The Vale of Eternity — Comprehensive Rulebook & Reference Guide

Welcome to the fantastical world of **The Vale of Eternity**, a strategic card-drafting and engine-building game for 2–4 players. As a powerful tamer, you will hunt, tame, and summon a diverse array of monsters, spirits, and gods from global myths, with the ultimate goal of capturing the noble and elusive dragons.

---

## 1. Game Specifications & Components

### Specifications
* **Age:** 14+
* **Player Count:** 2–4 players
* **Duration:** Approximately 40 minutes

### Components List
* **1 Game Board:** Features specialized zones for each of the creature families.
* **1 Eternity Standee:** A decorative piece placed in the center of the game board.
* **1 Score Board:** Tracks both the current round and player scores.
* **1 Round Marker:** Placed on the Round Track to track game progression.
* **1 First Player Marker:** Designates the active leader of the current round.
* **4 Player Tiles:** Double-sided tiles used to represent player color and handle scores exceeding 60 points.
* **12 Player Markers:** (3 markers in each of the 4 player colors) Used for drafting cards and score tracking.
* **42 Magic Stones:** The game currency, divided into three denominations:
  * **16 Red Stones:** Worth 1 currency unit each.
  * **14 Blue Stones:** Worth 3 currency units each.
  * **12 Purple Stones:** Worth 6 currency units each.
* **70 Creature Cards:** Unique cards representing mythical beings across 5 unique families.

---

## 2. Game Setup

Follow these steps in order to prepare the game:

1. **Player Components:** Each player chooses a color and takes 2 matching Player Markers, 1 Score Marker, and 1 Player Tile (placed front-side up).
2. **First Player Designation:** The player who has most recently seen a reptile is crowned the first player and receives the First Player Marker.
3. **Board Layout:** Place the Game Board and the Score Board in the middle of the table. You may optionally place the Eternity Standee in the center of the Game Board.
4. **Card Deck:** Shuffle all 70 Creature Cards thoroughly to form a face-down draw pile. Leave sufficient space next to it for a discard pile.
5. **Round Tracker:** Place the Round Marker on the first space ("1") of the Round Track.
6. **Turn Order Setup & Compensation:** Going clockwise from the first player, place each player's Score Marker on the Scoring Track. To offset turn order advantage, players receive starting victory points equal to their initial turn order rank:
   * **1st Player:** 1 Point
   * **2nd Player:** 2 Points
   * **3rd Player:** 3 Points
   * **4th Player:** 4 Points
7. **Resource Supply:** Place all Magic Stones next to the Score Board to form the general supply. Players do not start the game with any Magic Stones.

## 3. Core Anatomy of a Card

Every card represents a unique mythological creature with specific attributes. Understanding these regions is critical to executing valid actions.

### 1. Summoning Cost
Located in the upper-left corner below the family emblem, this numerical value dictates the exact amount of currency units required to summon the creature into your active Area.

### 2. Family Emblem & Type
Located in the topmost left corner, this colored icon indicates the creature's elemental or divine alignment. There are 5 distinct families:
* 🔥 **Fire:** Associated with cheap card actions, resource management, and Red Stones ultilization.
* 💧 **Water:** Centered around utility, resource generation, and Blue Stones ultilization.
* 🌿 **Earth:** Focuses on high-scoring capability, resource manipulation, high sell values, and Purple Stones ultilization.
* 💨 **Wind:** Grants hand manipulation, card advantageous, and general support.
* 🐉 **Dragon:** The ultimate high-tier creatures, demanding heavy investment but offering massive rewards.

### 3. Creature Name
The name of the mythical beast is clearly centered horizontally across the middle divider bar.

### 4. Effect Text & Ability Icons
The text box explains the unique rule modifications or benefits the creature provides. Every effect is preceded by a distinctive visual icon indicating its **Timing Type**:

* ⚡ **Instant Effect:** Triggers immediately and *only once* at the exact moment the card is successfully summoned.
* ♾️ **Permanent Effect:** Becomes active immediately upon summoning and remains continuously active as a passive modification to standard game rules.
* ⏳ **Active Effect:** Triggers precisely once per round during the dedicated **Resolution Phase**, executed in an order of the player's choosing.

## 4. Fundamental Game Rules & Mechanics

To ensure smooth gameplay, maintain structural compliance with these core resource constraints:

### The Magic Stone Rule (The Rule of 4)
* **Hard Storage Limit:** A player may **never have more than 4 physical Magic Stones** in their possession at any time, regardless of their denominations or value.
* **Instant Disposal Requirement:** If an action or effect would cause you to receive stones exceeding this capacity, you must immediately select exactly 4 stones to keep and discard the rest back to the supply. This clean-up decision must be completed *before* taking any subsequent actions.
* **No Public Change:** When paying for a card or action, players do not receive change from the supply if they overpay. For example, paying a Purple Stone (worth 6) for a cost-4 card results in forfeiting the extra 2 units.
* **Open Information:** All held Magic Stones must remain completely visible to all opponents at all times.
* **Infinite Supply:** The physical token pool is unlimited. If the supply runs out, use temporary proxies.

### The Player Area Rule (Round-Bound Capacity)
* **What is an Area?** Your Area is the display zone directly in front of you where your actively summoned creature cards reside.
* **Dynamic Capacity Limit:** The maximum number of cards you can have summoned in your Area is **strictly equal to the current round number**.
  * *Example:* In Round 1, you can possess a maximum of 1 summoned card. In Round 5, your capacity increases to 5 cards.
* **Full-Area Blockade:** If your current card count matches the current round number, your Area is full. You cannot summon any additional cards until an area is freed by removing an existing creature.

### Victory Point Trajectory
* Points are tracked instantly via the Scoring Track.
* If an effect reduces your score, adjust your marker backward. However, a player's score **can never fall below zero**.
* If a player's score matches or exceeds 60 points, immediately flip their Player Tile to the "+60" side to properly preserve tracking continuity.

## 5. Phase-by-Phase Gameplay Structure

The game is played over a series of successive rounds (maximum of 10). Each individual round is divided into 3 mandatory phases executed sequentially.

### Phase 1: Hunting Phase (Card Drafting)
During this phase, players scout and lock down wild mythical creatures.

1. **Card Revelation:** Reveal face-up cards from the draw pile equal to **twice the current player count** (e.g., reveal 8 cards in a 4-player game). Sort these cards by their respective families and position them into their designated matching zones flanking the main Game Board.
2. **First Draft Pass (Clockwise):** Beginning with the first player and moving clockwise, each player places one of their Player Markers on any available face-up card. A player *cannot* select a card that already possesses a marker.
3. **Second Draft Pass (Counter-Clockwise Snake):** Once the last player in turn order has placed their first marker, the drafting direction immediately reverses. The last player places their second marker on a remaining card (effectively taking two consecutive card draft choices). Drafting proceeds counter-clockwise, concluding back with the first player placing their final marker.
4. **Transition:** Once all revealed cards have exactly one Player Marker assigned, the phase immediately ends.

### Phase 2: Action Phase (Engine Execution)
Beginning with the first player and proceeding clockwise, each player takes their entire active turn in full. On your turn, you may perform **any number of actions** from the 4 primary types below, in any order, and may repeat them as many times as you can afford.

*You cannot voluntarily end your turn while any of your Player Markers remain on cards on the board; they must be fully resolved via Selling or Taming.*

#### Action A: Sell a Card
* **Declaration:** Select a card on the board that currently hosts one of your Player Markers.
* **Resolution:** Retrieve your Player Marker. Discard the selected card into the face-up discard pile.
* **Payout:** Receive Magic Stones from the general supply according to the **Family of the card**, completely ignoring its printed summoning cost. The default family payouts are:

  * 🌿 Earth Card $

  * 💨 Wind Card $

* *Remember:* You must apply the **Rule of 4** immediately upon receiving these stones.

#### Action B: Tame a Card
* **Declaration:** Select a card on the board hosting one of your Player Markers.
* **Resolution:** Retrieve your Player Marker and move the card directly into your private hand.
* *Hand Limit:* There is no limit to the number of cards you may hold in your hand. Hand contents must remain completely hidden from opponents.

#### Action C: Summon a Card
* **Declaration:** Choose a creature card directly from your private hand to bring into play.
* **Validation Check:** Verify that your Area is not currently full (Current active cards < Current round number).
* **Payment:** Total the printed Summoning Cost (modified by any active passive card effects in your Area). Return an equivalent or greater value of Magic Stones to the general supply. No change is given. Costs can stack and be reduced, but **can never fall below 0**.
* **Deployment:** Place the card face-up in your Area.
* **Immediate Trigger:** If the summoned card contains an **Instant Effect (⚡)**, you must fully resolve it immediately.

#### Action D: Remove a Card
* **Declaration:** Select a face-up card currently residing inside your *own* Area to destroy.
* **Cost Requirement:** You must pay a generic currency cost **strictly equal to the current round number** (e.g., removing a card in Round 4 costs 4 currency units). Pay this using your Magic Stones; no change is returned.
* **Resolution:** Discard the card into the global discard pile. This action immediately frees up an active structural slot in your Area.

### Phase 3: Resolution Phase (Engine Activation)
Once all players have concluded their individual Action Phase turns, players simultaneously transition into engine activation.

1. **Turn Execution:** Starting with the first player and moving clockwise, players take turns activating their engines.
2. **Active Effect Triggers:** On your turn, you must activate **every available Active Effect (⏳)** among the face-up cards in your Area exactly once.
3. **Strategic Sequencing:** If you possess multiple Active Effects, you have absolute freedom to sequence and execute them in whatever order you choose. Use this flexibility to maximize tactical synergies (see case studies below).
4. **Conclusion:** Once all players have evaluated their Active Effects, the Resolution Phase terminates.

### End of the Round Checklist
At the close of the Resolution Phase, perform the following housekeeping tasks:
1. **End Game Verification:** Check if an end-of-game trigger condition has been achieved. If yes, proceed straight to final scoring.
2. **Advance Time:** Move the Round Marker forward by one space on the Round Track.
3. **Pass Leadership:** The player sitting directly to the left of the current first player receives the First Player Marker, becoming the new first player for the upcoming round.

## 6. Golden Rules & Complex Interaction Case Studies

When playing, always obey the foundational priority rule: **If a text effect printed on a card directly conflicts with a standard written game rule, the card text takes absolute precedence.**

### Case Study 1: Total Resolution Compliance (The Medusa Clause)
* **Rule:** Every card effect must be executed to its maximum possible extent. You cannot optionally opt-out of a card's text.
* **Trigger Mechanics:** If a card effect uses conditional text or an exchange paradigm ("Do X to get Y"), **if you cannot completely fulfill the initial trigger condition (X), you are strictly forbidden from activating the resulting benefit (Y).**
* *Example Card (Medusa):* "⏳ Discard a card from your hand, then earn 1 Purple Stone."
  * *Scenario A:* If it is the Resolution Phase and you have cards in your hand, you *must* select one and discard it, then take your Purple Stone.
  * *Scenario B:* If your hand is entirely empty, you cannot fulfill the requirement to discard. Therefore, the effect fizzles completely, and you cannot gain the Purple Stone.

### Case Study 2: Structural Targeting Validity (The Ember Clause)
* **Rule:** A card cannot be summoned from your hand if any part of its mandatory Instant Effect is structurally impossible to fulfill at the moment of deployment.
* *Example Card (Ember):* "⚡ Earn 7 points. A player of your choice discards one of their summoned 💧 Water cards."
  * *Scenario A:* If at least one player at the table (including yourself) has an active, summoned Water creature in their Area, the action is legal. You pay the cost, deploy Ember, gain 7 points, and designate that player. That player chooses which of their specific Water cards to discard.
  * *Scenario B:* If absolutely no players at the entire table have a Water card deployed in their Area, **you are completely prohibited from summoning Ember**. It must remain in your hand until a valid target exists.

### Case Study 3: Self-Exclusion from Passives (The Kappa Clause)
* **Rule:** When deploying a card that features a Permanent passive effect (♾️) modifying summoning properties, that card's own deployment cost or immediate settlement *cannot* be affected by its own text. It must be fully in play first.
* *Example Card (Kappa):* "♾️ Whenever you summon a card using a Blue Stone, earn 2 points."
  * *Scenario:* You pay for Kappa using a combination that includes a Blue Stone. You do *not* earn 2 points for this payment, because Kappa's permanent text tracking is not online until after it has been fully placed into the Area. Any *subsequent* cards summoned using Blue Stones will trigger the bonus.

### Case Study 4: Immediate Presence Awareness (The Eternity Clause)
* **Rule:** When evaluating a card's Instant Effect (⚡) at the moment of summoning, the card itself is considered to have already successfully crossed the border into your Area. It is no longer considered to be in your hand.
* *Example Card (Eternity):* "⚡ Earn 4 points for each unique card family present in your area."
  * *Scenario:* You summon Eternity (a Dragon family card) into an empty Area. When resolving this effect, Eternity counts itself. Since the Dragon family is actively present in your Area via its own physical card body, you immediately secure 4 points.

### Case Study 5: Active Engine Loops (The Imp Clause)
* **Keyword Definition:** **Recover** is a specialized capability that removes a card from its active slot in your Area and returns it safely back into your private hand. If no external target is specified, the card targets itself.
* *Example Card (Imp):* Contains two separate text components: "⚡ Earn 2 Red Stones" and "⏳ Recover."
  * *Engine Application:* In the Action Phase, you summon Imp, immediately gaining 2 Red Stones (Instant). Later, during the Resolution Phase, its Active effect triggers: Imp returns itself to your hand. In the next round's Action Phase, you can fully repeat this loop, effectively turning Imp into a repeatable resource generation engine.

### Case Study 6: Sequencing Mastery (Odin & Griffon Interaction)
Strategic sequencing during the Resolution Phase separates novice tamers from master tacticians. Consider this scenario:
* **Your Current Hand Size:** 5 cards.
* **Your Deployed Active Cards:**
  1. **Griffon:** "⏳ Draw a card."
  2. **Odin:** "⏳ If you have less than 6 cards in your hand, earn 2 Red Stones. Otherwise, earn 1 Purple Stone."

You have two entirely distinct choices for sequencing your turn during Phase 3:

* **Path A (Resource Generation):** You choose to activate **Odin** first. The game checks your hand size (5 cards). Because 5 is less than 6, you successfully earn 2 Red Stones. Next, you activate **Griffon**, drawing 1 card to bring your hand total to 6.
* **Path B (Value Maximization):** You choose to activate **Griffon** first. You draw 1 card, increasing your current hand size from 5 to 6 cards. Next, you activate **Odin**. The game checks your hand size (6 cards). Because your hand size is no longer less than 6, you trigger the alternate condition, earning a highly valuable Purple Stone (worth 6 currency units).

## 7. End of Game & Final Victory Conditions

The end of the game is triggered immediately when either of the following milestones is met:
1. **Score Trigger:** Any player achieves a score of **60 or more points** at the conclusion of any full round.
2. **Time Trigger:** The **10th round** officially concludes.

### Determining the Winner
The player who has accumulated the **highest total number of points** on the Scoring Track is declared the master tamer and wins the game.

### Tie-Breaker Hierarchies
If multiple players are locked in a tie for the highest score, apply the following tie-breaker metrics in order:
1. **Area Density:** The tied player who possesses the **most face-up summoned cards** inside their active Area wins.
2. **Shared Glory:** If the card count is identical, the tie cannot be broken. The tied players share the victory equally.
